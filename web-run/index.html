Two tiers

VerifiedReadings climbed steadily from 50% or less, with no gap over 20 minutes, and real tokens in the local logs.

StandardA believable run that misses one of those checks — joined mid-window, say, or the Mac slept.

How it works, and how it stays honest

Track on QuotaBar, compete on Quota Run. The short version: nothing leaves your Mac until you say so, and nobody gets to report their own score.

Local first

Personal records need no account. QuotaBar keeps every run on your Mac — fastest to 100%, time to 50% and 90%, highest peak — whether or not you ever join.

Opt-in, in writing

Upload stays off until you join, and joining shows exactly what will leave the Mac before anything does. Leave Quota Run and every row about you is deleted from the server.

Readings, not results

The app sends raw readings; the server works out every run and every rank. A client that claims “100% in two hours” is simply ignored.

Uploaded after you join

  • Provider and plan name
  • Used percent, reset time and reading time for each window
  • The window's length and scope
  • A one-way digest of the provider account, never the email
  • Tokens per minute from local CLI logs — counts only

Never uploaded

  • Credentials, tokens, cookies, API keys
  • Prompts, code, model outputs
  • File and project paths
  • Your account email in the clear

One ranked Mac

Each Mac signs its readings with a key that never leaves it, and an account ranks readings from one Mac only. Moving the ranking to another Mac waits seven days, and a provider account can belong to one runner.

Checked, then tiered

Readings have to rise steadily and plausibly. Runs with a gap, or a late start, count as Standard; readings that drop or leap, or an account seen under two runners, are held back for review and never shown.

No prizes

No money, no rewards. A desktop client can't be made impossible to cheat, only expensive and visible — so all there is to win is the bragging and a profile to show what you built.
